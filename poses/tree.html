How to improve your Tree Pose

Standing leg strong, hips level, torso tall and centered, head steady. Here’s the rubric Posewise measures it against, and what to train when you miss.

The rubric

Posewise checks four things from the front. Here’s what good looks like.

  • Standing legStraight and strong, knee not locked165–185°hip · knee · ankle
  • HipsLevel, neither side hiking or dropping0–7°tilt
  • TorsoTall and centered over the standing foot0–12°lean from vertical
  • HeadLevel, gaze on one still point0–9°tilt

What’s holding it back

Strengthen your outer hips, and your hips will stay level.

The standing-leg glutes hold the pelvis level on one leg. When they’re weak, the hips tip and the torso leans to compensate.

  • Clamshells12 a side
  • Lateral step-outs10 a side

Level your hips and stand tall over your foot.

Build quad strength, and your standing leg will stay straight and strong.

A standing knee that softens as you hold usually points to single-leg strength running out.

  • Quad sets12 a side
  • Step-downs8 a side

Press down through your standing foot.

And one cue: fix your gaze on one still point ahead and keep your head level. A steady gaze steadies everything below it.

Quick answers

Can I put my foot on my knee?

No. Place it above or below the knee, on your inner thigh or calf, so there’s no sideways pressure on the joint.

Why do I lose my balance?

Usually a wandering gaze or a weak standing hip. Fix your eyes on one still point and press your foot and inner leg into each other.

Why does my foot slide down?

Press your foot and leg into each other with equal force, or start with your foot on your calf or your toes on the floor.

Should my hips be level?

Yes. The lifted-leg hip tends to hike up, so draw it down until both hip points face forward and sit level.

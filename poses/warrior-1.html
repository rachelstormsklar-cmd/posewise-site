How to improve your Warrior I

Front knee over the ankle at a right angle, back heel down, hips facing forward, arms reaching straight up. Here’s the rubric Posewise measures it against, and what to train when you miss.

What’s holding it back

Build quad and glute strength, and your front knee will reach a 90° bend.

The shape is there, the depth isn’t. That usually points to leg strength, not technique.

  • Split squats8 a side
  • Goblet squats12 reps

Bend your front knee toward 90°, right over your ankle.

Lengthen your calves, and your back heel will stay down and your back leg will straighten.

With the back heel grounded, tight calves usually lift the heel or pull the back knee into a bend. Shorten your stance until they ease.

  • Wall calf stretch20s a side
  • Ankle mobility drill10 a side

Press your back heel down into the mat.

Lengthen your hip flexors, and your torso will stay tall and your hips will turn toward the front.

Tight hip flexors on the back leg tip the pelvis forward and pull that hip open, so the chest leans and the hips twist.

  • Low lunge stretch30s a side
  • Figure-four stretch30s a side

Draw your back hip forward and lift your chest.

Strengthen your outer hips, and your front knee will track over your toes.

When the outer glutes are weak, the front knee caves in toward the big toe.

  • Clamshells12 a side
  • Lateral step-outs10 a side

Track your front knee out over your middle toe.

Free up your shoulders, and your arms will reach straight up, in line with your ears.

When shoulder range runs out overhead, the arms angle forward, one side rides higher, or the shoulders creep toward the ears.

  • Wall slides10 reps
  • Doorway lat stretch20s a side

Reach your arms up in line with your ears, through your fingertips.

Quick answers

Should my back heel be down?

Yes. Turn your back foot out about 45° and press the heel into the mat. If it won’t stay down, shorten your stance; a lifted heel turns the pose into Crescent Lunge.

Do my hips need to be square?

Point them forward as far as is comfortable, but most bodies can’t fully square them with the back heel down. Stepping your feet a little wider apart, hip-width rather than heel to heel, helps.

Why does my lower back hurt?

Usually tight hip flexors on the back leg tip the pelvis forward and arch the low back. Shorten your stance and draw your tailbone down as you lift your chest.

What’s the difference between Warrior I and Warrior II?

Warrior I faces the front with your arms overhead. Warrior II opens your hips and chest to the side with your arms out at shoulder height.

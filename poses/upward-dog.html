How to improve your Upward Dog

Arms straight, shoulders over wrists, chest lifted, thighs off the mat. Here’s the rubric Posewise measures it against, and what to train when you miss.

The rubric

Posewise checks six things from the side. Here’s what good looks like.

  • ArmsStraight, without lockingStraightshoulder · elbow · wrist
  • HipsLifted, the bend spread up your back125–180°shoulder · hip · knee
  • LegsStraight and active, thighs off the matStraighthip · knee · ankle
  • ShouldersStacked over your wristsStackedshoulder over wrist
  • NeckLong, chest leading, not chin145–185°hip · shoulder · ear
  • WristsWeight across the whole palmEvenwrist bend

What’s holding it back

Build pressing strength, and your arms will straighten and hold your chest up.

When the arms can’t yet carry you, the elbows bend and the chest sinks back toward Cobra.

  • Push-up hold30s
  • Prone chest lifts10 reps

Press the floor away to straighten your arms.

Open your upper back, and the bend will spread up your spine instead of pinching your low back.

A stiff upper back, often with tight hip flexors, lets the hips sink and the bend collect in the low back.

  • Upper-back extensions8–10 reps
  • Low lunge stretch30s a side

Lift through your chest, not your low back.

Build glute and leg strength, and your thighs will lift off the mat.

When the legs go slack, the thighs drop and the low back takes the load.

  • Prone leg lifts12 a side
  • Quad sets12 a side

Press the tops of your feet down and lift your thighs.

Free up your shoulders, and your shoulders will stack over your wrists.

Tight lats or a tired core let the shoulders drift forward of the hands and hunch toward the ears.

  • Lat stretch20s a side
  • Plank to up dog8 slow reps

Shift your shoulders back over your wrists.

And two cues: lead with your chest, not your chin, so the back of your neck stays long, and spread your fingers to press through your whole palm.

Quick answers

What’s the difference between Upward Dog and Cobra?

In Upward Dog your arms are straight and your hips and thighs lift off the mat, so only your hands and the tops of your feet touch. In Cobra your hips stay down and your elbows stay bent.

Should my thighs touch the floor?

No. Press the tops of your feet down and lift your thighs. If you can’t yet, do Cobra instead.

Why does my lower back hurt?

Usually the hips sag and the bend collects in the low back. Firm your legs, draw your shoulders back over your wrists and lift through your chest, or switch to Cobra.

Where should I look?

Straight ahead or slightly up, with your neck long. Avoid dropping your head back.
